  Decision 1: how we represent amounts

  - A. BigInt in smallest units (recommended): parse the string into {units:
    bigint, decimals} and format back to a string, in our own ~80 lines in
    src/money/.
    - For: exact, no dependency, works on web, easy to test, and floats can't
      sneak in.
    - Against: we write and test parsing and formatting ourselves. The USD total
      multiplies two scales (amount × price), which needs care.
  - B. A decimal library (big.js, about 6KB):
    - For: well tested, has rounding modes.
    - Against: another dependency to audit for web support. We still have to
      write strict input parsing, and toNumber() is one call away.
  - C. Keep strings and let the server do the math:
    - For: least code.
    - Against: we can't show the fee/receive amount or check the minimum and
      available without math. Rejected.

  Decision 2: the balance right after a claim

  - A. Optimistic overlay, cleared by asOf:
    - How: on 201, show the asset's available as snapshot − amount with a
      "Claiming 0.5 ETH…" row. The overlay is removed only once a snapshot
      arrives with asOf ≥ claim.createdAt, because that snapshot already
      includes the claim. That avoids the "dropped, then jumped back" problem.
      If the claim fails, we show "Failed: reason, 0.5 ETH returned", so the
      number going back up has an explanation.
    - For: fixes both complaints.
    - Against: client-side money math, and it relies on our reading of how
      snapshots and asOf behave (which is how the docs describe it). It's a pure
      function we can unit-test.
  - B. Server numbers only, plus a visible pending state:
    - How: never change the numbers locally. Show a "Claim in progress: 0.5 ETH,
      pending" badge on the asset and block a second claim of that asset until
      the first settles, then refetch.
    - For: simplest, never shows a number the server didn't send.
    - Against: available looks unchanged for up to 15s. The badge and the block
      address the "claimed again" complaint, but a user could still be confused
      for that window.
  - C. Server numbers plus a separate "after pending claims" line: keep the
    server's available as is, and show an estimated "≈ 0.32 after claim" line
    underneath until the snapshot catches up.
    - For: honest about what's estimated.
    - Against: two numbers on a 390px screen, and as much code as A.

  I'd pick A if the claim flow lands quickly and B if we're short on time. Your
  call.

  My suggestion for an unknown claim result (504, network error, 5xx, timeout)

  - Idempotency key: generated once per confirmed request and reused for every
    retry. The form is locked while the outcome is unknown, so the amount can't
    be changed and a second claim can't be created under a new key.
  - Retries: on 504, network failure or 500/503, show "Checking your claim…"
    (not "failed") and automatically repeat the same POST with the same key 2–3
    times with backoff. A 429 waits retryAfterMs. A replay (200) or 201 gives us
    the claim id.
  - Still unknown after that: show "We couldn't confirm your claim. Don't claim
    again, it may still go through," with a "Check again" button that repeats
    the POST with the same key.
  - Once we have an id: poll GET /claims/{id} every 2s. After about 30s, show
    "Taking longer than usual" and let the user refresh by hand.
  - Definite outcomes: 400 means a real rejection with readable text, and the
    form unlocks. 401 goes to the token screen. 409 shows a message only.
  - Risk: reloading the page drops the key. If there's time, I'll keep the
    in-flight attempt in localStorage.

  How it works:
  - The saved request now keeps the time of its first POST.
  - "Check again" re-sends the same key only within 23 hours of that time (an
    hour of margin under your 24h).
  - After that, or for an old saved request with no time:
    - The app sends no POST and shows "We can't safely re-check this claim",
      with the original amount and date.
    - It tells the user to look at their balance before claiming again, and
      offers "I've checked, dismiss".
  - This is in useClaimSubmission.ts (src/hooks/useClaimSubmission.ts),
    claimIntent.ts (src/hooks/claimIntent.ts) and ClaimProgress.tsx
    (src/components/ClaimProgress.tsx).

    find a claim by idempotency key.
